# 03 — User Personas

> Not demographics. Real people.
> Every design decision has a reason because we know who we're designing for.

---

## 1. Primary Persona — Karabelo

### Who he is
- Engineering student
- Loves beautiful, well-crafted technology
- Enjoys deep Bible discussions
- Likes interactive experiences
- Prefers calm over flashy

### His goals
- Have meaningful family worship with his partner
- Use technology that feels premium and intentional
- Grow spiritually through guided discussion
- Create a lasting habit of worship together

### His frustrations
- Apps that feel like games, not worship
- Cluttered, noisy interfaces
- Content that talks down to him
- Sessions that feel rushed or shallow

### What he needs
- A beautiful, peaceful experience
- Deep, thoughtful discussion prompts
- A sense of craftsmanship in every detail
- The ability to worship remotely with his partner

### Design implications
- Premium, polished UI (storybook aesthetic)
- Deep, open-ended questions
- Subtle, meaningful animations (not flashy)
- Remote sync that "just works"

---

## 2. Primary Persona — Yolanda

### Who she is
- Enjoys meaningful conversations
- Wants to grow spiritually
- Prefers encouraging experiences
- Likes beautiful visuals
- Values emotional connection

### Her goals
- Feel closer to Jehovah and to Karabelo
- Have warm, honest conversations
- Experience worship that feels personal, not generic
- Feel encouraged, never pressured

### Her frustrations
- Content that feels preachy or robotic
- Questions that feel like quizzes
- Experiences that feel cold or impersonal
- Being rushed or graded

### What she needs
- Warm, gentle language
- Questions that invite sharing, not testing
- Beautiful, calming visuals
- A sense of safety and openness

### Design implications
- Warm, encouraging tone throughout
- Open-ended, personal questions
- Soft, beautiful color palette
- No scores, no timers, no pressure

---

## 3. Secondary Persona — Parent & Child (Age 8–12)

### Who they are
- A parent who wants to do family worship with their child
- A child who loves stories, pictures, and play

### Their goals
- Learn Bible stories in a fun, engaging way
- Have quality time together
- Build a foundation of faith

### Their frustrations
- Content that's too complex or abstract
- Long text blocks
- Questions the child can't relate to

### What they need
- Simpler questions
- More illustrations
- More animations
- Shorter sessions
- Visual storytelling

### Design implications
- Future "Family Mode" with simplified content
- More visual/illustration-heavy presentation
- Shorter, more playful activities
- Age-appropriate language

---

## 4. Tertiary Persona — The Curious Visitor

### Who they are
- Someone who discovers Heart to Heart through a friend or link
- Not sure what to expect
- Values quality and beauty

### Their goals
- Understand what this is quickly
- Have a delightful first impression
- Decide if it's worth using

### Their frustrations
- Confusing onboarding
- Cluttered first screen
- No clear sense of value

### What they need
- A clear, beautiful welcome
- Immediate sense of purpose
- Easy to start

### Design implications
- The Welcome screen must be stunning and clear
- "Start Worship" is the only obvious action
- No clutter, no confusion
